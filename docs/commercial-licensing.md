# Commercial licensing and project funding

Policy adopted on 13 September 2026 following the owner's instruction to assess
and implement a sustainable commercial option. This supersedes the earlier policy
against a commercial relicensing program, not the AGPL license or existing grants.
**Status: program policy adopted; commercial licenses are not yet offered.**

## What DAIA offers

DAIA Community remains the complete public core under AGPL-3.0-or-later, with no
license fee. Commercial use and paid hosting are permitted under that license.
Internal use does not by itself create a payment obligation. Sharing within one
legal entity differs from distribution to other entities; modified network use
must also be assessed under section 13, including internal network users.

The commercial option is an alternative license for specifically identified DAIA
code and versions, allowing agreed proprietary modifications or incorporation
without that code's AGPL obligations. It is not a fee for using DAIA at work.
Separate programs and data do not automatically require such an exception.
Affiliates, contractors, redistribution and OEM use must be specified in a contract;
no universal corporate-group permission or third-party license waiver is promised.
Support, managed hosting and deployment services can be sold separately and need
not require proprietary code. None of these services is currently promised here.

## Community guarantee

The official project must continue to make the core code it commercially licenses
available under AGPL-3.0-or-later. Commercial licensing must not be used to withdraw
that edition or revoke previously granted AGPL rights. Project-owned core changes
included in an alternative-licensed release must also be published in the AGPL
edition. Customers' own private changes need not be contributed upstream unless
agreed separately; customer data and independent deliverables are not included.
A project policy cannot compel future development or ensure perpetual hosting.

This guarantee must be included in executed contributor grants and any successor
steward agreement, not merely stated on a website. Existing AGPL grants remain
subject to their existing terms. Donations alone never purchase an exception.

## Interim stewardship

The founding human operator has accepted interim stewardship and intends to license
the rights they hold personally. Public materials may identify this person by their
project role; legal identity must be disclosed to contractual counterparties and
verified in the private agreement records. No foundation exists by virtue of this
policy. A later foundation requires a separate documented transfer or license of
rights and assumption of the community obligations; ownership does not move merely
because an organization is formed.

This appointment establishes operational responsibility and the intended licensor,
not exclusive ownership of all code or a transfer of contributors' rights. The
founder's existing rights, where they exist, remain theirs. The private stewardship
record does not substitute for release-specific rights clearance.

## Authority before issuing any commercial license

DAIA is a project name, not a legal person. Operational control of an account,
repository or agent is not ownership of every contribution. Neither sanitized Git
metadata nor an AI provider's output terms establishes an exclusive copyright
chain for the whole repository. Employer rights, copied material, dependencies,
AI-assisted provenance and existing contributions need separate examination.

Before making a binding offer or issuing an alternative license, the maintainer
must record all of the following:

1. An identified legal licensor and a person authorized to sign for it. Keep legal
   identity and contractual details in restricted records, not public commits.
2. A rights review of the exact release: ownership or an adequate express grant for
   every included original contribution, and applicable third-party permissions.
   Unknown or AGPL-only material cannot be silently included in the exception.
3. Executed supplemental grants where needed, including explicit authority for
   commercial sublicensing and the community guarantee. No retroactive assent.
4. A reviewed commercial agreement defining covered version/artifacts, customer,
   scope, rights, fees, support if any, liability, applicable law and termination.
   Third-party licenses continue to apply independently.
5. A lawful receiving/contracting arrangement and approval of its tax, accounting
   and fiscal-host implications. A fiscal host does not gain licensing authority
   simply by receiving money. No provider or host is appointed by this document.

Use the [rights register procedure](licensing-rights-register.md) and the
[supplemental grant draft](contributor-commercial-grant.md). These documents prepare
execution; they do not assert that anyone has already signed or transferred rights.

## Project funds

All receipts from official commercial licensing are allocated to DAIA's project
budget, subject to tax and contractual obligations. This is an accounting policy,
not the creation of a trust, foundation or legal owner called “DAIA Fund.” It does
not require existing personal funds or other accounts to be transferred.

Permitted project expenses include infrastructure, security review, legal/accounting
work, development, contributor bounties and reasonable compensation for documented
maintenance. Compensation is payment for work, not a founder entitlement or profit
distribution. No salary, percentage, spending authority or recurring payment is
created here. Existing approval requirements for expenditure still apply.

Record gross receipts, tax, payment/host fees, expenses and reserves. Publish useful
aggregates and decisions while protecting personal, customer and contract data.
Disclose conflicts of interest; a recipient must not be the sole approver of their
own compensation. A reserve and compensation budget should follow actual costs
and available funds rather than an invented fixed percentage. Donor restrictions
and any fiscal-host terms must be honored.

## Assessment and sources

The proposal fits the mission because organizations can buy an alternative license
while participants retain an open core. It cannot force a lawful AGPL competitor to
pay, prevent an independent implementation, or establish future demand. Revenue
examples are scenarios, not forecasts. Licensing freedom does not replace the work
needed to build a useful and safe product.

- [GNU FAQ: parallel licenses](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.en.html#HeardOtherLicense)
  and [internal distribution](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.en.html#InternalDistribution).
- [AGPLv3, especially sections 2, 10 and 13](https://opensource.org/license/agpl-3.0).
- [Apache's contributor-agreement guidance](https://apache.org/licenses/contributor-agreements.html)
  illustrates explicit authority and employer rights; its agreement is not adopted
  as a DAIA dual-licensing contract.
- [Open Source Europe application and terms](https://opencollective.com/europe/apply)
  retain project IP and require project-related spending. Published fees were 8%
  plus processor fees for online contributions and 10% for bank transfers and
  specified grants on this review date. They are not a DAIA quote or agreement;
  commercial contracting and compensation eligibility require confirmation.
