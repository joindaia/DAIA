# DAIA writing

Write for a reader deciding whether this project interests them and whether to
contribute. Give them enough substance to form that judgment. English is the default.
This skill has no outside company's profile, sales funnel or required headline style.

## The project behind the words

DAIA is being built so people can contribute their own AI agents to shared software
and research projects. Its ambition includes distributing planning and responsibility,
so a project can continue without one person coordinating every step. Developing
DAIA itself is the first practical use. A collective attempt at a Millennium Prize
Problem is a longer-term ambition; authorized bug bounties and customer projects are
possible routes to paid work. The proposed reward model includes contributors and a
disclosed share for the platform. These intentions do not establish present capability. Explain their status without
shrinking the project to its current test workload.

When working in the DAIA repository, consult the relevant current source: README.md
for implementation, docs/roadmap.md for milestones, docs/research-mission.md and
docs/paid-work.md for research and rewards, or docs/public-launch.md for participation
and isolation plans. Read only what the page needs. If those sources are unavailable,
use the supplied brief and identify any material missing facts. Do not invent them.

## Develop the meaning before fitting the page

Decide what the reader should understand by the end and what information would make
that understanding possible. Develop the relationship between the ideas: why this
project exists, how a contribution could matter, and what the reader could do next.
An example should reveal something about that relationship. Merely saying that tasks
are assigned, findings recorded and results checked does not explain DAIA's purpose.

Draft connected paragraphs before turning them into page sections. Let headings name
what the paragraphs actually discuss. Change or remove a section when it forces a
pointless heading, repeated explanation or unnecessary call to action. A page does
not need a prescribed number of steps, features, slogans, FAQs or assurances.

Read adjacent pages so each has a reason to exist. The homepage introduces the idea;
a roadmap explains how it will develop; a participation page helps someone decide
what taking part involves. Those roles can guide the writing without becoming a
mandatory template. Technical instructions still need precise steps and warnings.

## Read back as a whole

Can a reader explain what DAIA is trying to make possible after reading the page?
Where does an example lead to the next idea? Which sentences could be pasted into
almost any agent product without changing their meaning? Revise those passages using
missing context or a clearer relationship, rather than simply stronger adjectives.

Read each heading together with the paragraph it introduces. If the heading turns
an ordinary situation into a staged reveal, describe the situation directly.
Judge rhythm and emphasis in context. A short sentence can work; so can a long one.
Do not treat a rejected phrase as a universal ban or substitute a new pair of punchy
slogans for an explanation. Meaning should survive when the typography is removed.

Keep ambition and current capability intelligible without surrounding every paragraph
with caveats. Put conditions where they affect the reader's decision. Never invent
results, provider approval, savings, availability or reward entitlements. Examples
must be recognizable as examples; distinguish them from work that actually happened.

## Improve the skill from use

A writing review should identify a passage, explain the reading it invites and propose
a revision with a reason. It may find no necessary change. Try a proposed skill change
on a different passage before treating it as reusable guidance. Do not accumulate
word bans or fixed sentence patterns from isolated feedback.

Agent review can reveal ambiguity or factual drift. Neither agreement between models
nor a structural check proves that readers find a page meaningful. Only mark an
example as editorially approved when the user has accepted it. Preserve that distinction
when recording DAIA writing-task results; this skill grants no publication authority.
